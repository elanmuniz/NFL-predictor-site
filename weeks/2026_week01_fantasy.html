Reading the table

Proj is expected PPR points. Floor – ceiling is the range the player lands in about 70% of weeks (15th–85th percentile of past outcomes for similar projections); the tick marks the projection.

Matchup grade compares the projection with the same player facing an average defense, with an average team total, at a neutral site. The change is measured against at least a typical fantasy starter's score (16 pts QB, 12 RB/WR, 9 TE, 8 K, 7 D/ST), so backups can't earn big grades from tiny swings: A+ is +15% or more, A +8%, B +3%, C roughly neutral, D −3%, F −8% or worse. Hide backups (on by default) shows depth-chart starters plus anyone ranked as a fantasy starter. The rank shows how that defense stacks up against the position (1st = allows the most points above expectation). This season's games count three times as much as last season's, and last season stops counting after six games.

Team pts is the Vegas-implied total for the player's team (for a D/ST, the opponent's implied points — fewer is better). Q = questionable (Out and Doubtful players are left off). New = little NFL history, so the projection leans on what players at that depth-chart spot usually score. Once a game kicks off, its projections are locked and Actual fills in after the final whistle.

Kickers and D/ST have their own tabs (they're left out of Overall and FLEX) and use standard scoring. Kickers: PAT +1, FG 0–39 yds +3, 40–49 +4, 50+ +5, missed FG or PAT −1. D/ST: sack +1, interception or fumble recovery +2, safety or blocked kick +2, defensive or return TD +6, and points allowed 0 → +10, 1–6 → +7, 7–13 → +4, 14–20 → +1, 21–27 → 0, 28–34 → −1, 35+ → −4. A kicker's matchup is the opposing defense and the game's venue (domes help); a D/ST's matchup is the opposing offense, and its Opp number is how many points that offense is expected to score.
